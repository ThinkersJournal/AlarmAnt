## Verdict

**Outside as read, and the escalation element does not read as claimed.** Claim 19 is the broadest Masimo claim yet: it drops the received / viewed / declined triple the granted claims turn on and keeps one negative reply ("has not accepted or is unavailable") that triggers an immediate escalation. AlarmAnt has no such reply and no escalation in response to a reply: it escalates only when no reply arrives, on a fixed timer. Two further elements are absent: the clinical alarm source and the patient-device or monitoring-computer sender. **This is the closest Masimo claim to our design, and it holds only while constraint 13 below holds.** No element of claim 19 that the design turns on is present, so this is a [READY], not an [ASK].

**Does the escalation element still read?** The act of reaching a second person reads in function, as it did against published claim 1 of US20150106121A1. The claim's trigger does not: it needs a received reply from the phone saying the provider has not accepted or is unavailable, and escalation "in response to the reply". A timer that expires with no reply is not a received reply. As read, 19f fails on both its trigger and its "immediately", and 19e is the element that does the work.

Other messages the app sends the server, checked against 19d/19e: `POST /register` (carries only the push token, before any alert), `PUT /device/token` (planned; carries a refreshed token), self-test acknowledgements (A6, an Acknowledge). None is a reply to an alarm notification and none says "not accepted" or "unavailable". Messages that come to us from the push service (FCM accepted, FCM `UNREGISTERED`) come from Google, not from the alarm notification component, and nothing escalates sooner on them today.

## Design constraints this reading adds

13. **No negative reply from the phone, and no escalation in response to a phone message.** The app never sends "not accepted", "unavailable", "busy", "snooze", "can't take it", "notifications off" or any status that the server uses to escalate. Escalation stays a fixed timer on the absence of an Acknowledge, never sooner. Watch two places where this could slip in by accident: slice (h) (the app reporting "unregistered" or "permissions missing" to the server) and any "push failed, escalate now" shortcut driven by an FCM error. Telling the **operator** (audit event, local screen) is fine; telling the escalator is not.
14. **No app-whitelisting or kiosk mode** for the phone (claim 22).
15. **No care-provider groups, rosters or location rules** for choosing person 2 (claims 36, 37, 38); extends constraint 3.
16. **Nothing in the repo or its docs describes AlarmAnt as a clinical or patient-monitoring alarm.** Claim 19b is clinical; the code and docs are generic by design. A new sample, doc or example must not introduce a physiological parameter or a patient.
